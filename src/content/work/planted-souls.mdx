## The brief

Planted Souls needed a website that matched a brand living mostly in its
founder's head. The real design problem was extraction before execution:
getting the brand out of conversation and into a form a build could follow.

The palette below isn't a proposal, it's what came off the vision board and
into the final screens: the marigold background, the forest-green headline
ink, the sage buttons, the terracotta of the pots themselves.

## How it went

<div class="planted-process">
  <div class="planted-card">
    <span class="pc-step">Step one</span>
    <h3>Vision board first</h3>
    <p>Before a single screen, we built a shared Figma board of brand colours, type directions, and references, reviewed live with the CEO until "that's it" replaced "sort of."</p>
  </div>
  <div class="planted-card">
    <span class="pc-step">Step two</span>
    <h3>High-fidelity mockups</h3>
    <p>I translated that direction into complete page layouts using the agreed colours, typography, imagery, and visual style, so we could review real screens instead of abstract descriptions.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="planted-card">
    <span class="pc-step">Step three</span>
    <h3>The navigation pass</h3>
    <p>We walked through the existing site's dead ends and ambiguous labels together, then reorganized the experience around how a first-time visitor would explore, learn, and get started.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout kind="method">
Working founder-to-designer taught me to treat alignment as a deliverable with
its own artifacts. The vision board mattered as much as the mockups it
produced.
</Callout>

## What stuck with me

Small-client work is a masterclass in listening. The CEO knew the brand better
than I ever could. My job was building the instrument that let that knowledge
become a website.
